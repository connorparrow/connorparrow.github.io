Research interests

Numerical methods for coupled PDE systems

My work centers on the design and analysis of partitioned numerical methods for multiphysics problems, particularly systems that couple fluid flow with deformable, porous, or multiphase media. Below is a short overview of my current areas of focus.

Stability and error analysis of partitioned schemes

A central part of my work is establishing the mathematical properties of the methods I design. I work on proving stability (unconditional or conditional, depending on the degree of partitioning), analyzing convergence rates, and identifying how choices like time discretization or subiteration structure affect accuracy and computational cost.

Time-stepping schemes for multiphysics coupling

I am interested in how the formulation of individual subproblems and the structure of the time-stepping scheme affect the overall method — for example, refactorizations of Cauchy-type schemes that reach second-order accuracy through recursive correction, and dual-mixed formulations that preserve mass conservation across a fluid-poroelastic interface.

Fluid-poroelastic structure interaction

Fluid-poroelastic structure interaction (FPSI) models the coupling of the time-dependent Stokes equations with the Biot equations of poroelasticity, with applications in biological systems, biomedical engineering, and geophysics. I develop and analyze Robin-Robin partitioned schemes for FPSI, including methods that fully partition the system into mechanics, Darcy-flow, and fluid subproblems, so that each physical process can be solved with a smaller, more manageable system while preserving the interactions between them.

Broader applications

Beyond FPSI, I am extending these partitioned-method techniques to other coupled PDE systems, including a project on two-phase flow using a coupled Cahn-Hilliard-Navier-Stokes system. More broadly, I am interested in problems involving fluid dynamics, multiphase flow, porous-media flow, elasticity, and other biologically or geophysically motivated PDE models. I am especially interested in problems where coupling different physical processes raises open questions about stability, accuracy, conservation, or computational efficiency.
